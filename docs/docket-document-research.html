Document Requirements: Research Findings & Industry Expansion

Answers to the eight open questions, researched against regulatory and lender sources — plus what Docket's workflow library should cover beyond lending.

Researched 1 August 2026 · Nothing here has been applied to the live checklist · Labels: regulatory source industry/secondary source

Two findings change things materially, and neither was on your list of questions.

1. Demanding "Aadhaar" as a required document is a compliance risk. The Supreme Court struck down Section 57 of the Aadhaar Act, ending private entities' power to compel Aadhaar. RBI's framework accepts any one of six Officially Valid Documents. Our checklist names Aadhaar specifically and marks it required — it should be "Identity proof (any one OVD)" with Aadhaar as one accepted option.

2. Demanding audited financials from everyone is unrealistic and often impossible. A proprietorship under ₹1 crore turnover has no legal obligation to be audited, so a compliant small business simply cannot produce the document we insist on.

Q1 · Is the current 9-item baseline right at all?

Verdict: the categories are right; the execution has three real defects.

Every lender source I checked asks for the same four buckets we do — identity KYC, address, business existence, financials. Nothing in our list is spurious. But three problems surfaced:

Defect 1 — "Aadhaar" should not be a named mandatory item regulatory

RBI's KYC Master Direction accepts any one of six OVDs: passport, driving licence, proof of possession of Aadhaar, voter ID, NREGA job card, or the NPR letter — with PAN (or Form 60) in addition. Meanwhile the Supreme Court struck down Section 57 of the Aadhaar Act, which is what had let private entities compel Aadhaar authentication; after that judgment Aadhaar is voluntary for private contracts.

Fix: rename the item to "Identity proof (any one Officially Valid Document)", listing all six in the description. This is also better for the AI classifier — today a borrower who sends a passport instead of Aadhaar has it filed as address proof or left unmatched.

Defect 2 — entity documents are incomplete lender sources

We ask a Private Limited company only for its Certificate of Incorporation. Lender checklists consistently also require MOA and AOA, and a CA/CS-certified list of directors and shareholders. For an LLP, the LLP Agreement is required — we don't ask for it at all, only the incorporation certificate. And a proprietorship typically signs a Sole Proprietorship Declaration, which we have no item for.

Defect 3 — no board resolution / authority to borrow

For companies and LLPs, lenders require a resolution authorising the borrowing and naming signatories. We never ask.

What this means in practice: a Pvt Ltd applicant completing our checklist 100% would still be missing MOA/AOA, the director list and a board resolution — so credit-ops would be chasing three documents outside the system, which is precisely the chaos Docket exists to remove.

Q2 · Are audited financials realistic for every applicant?

Verdict: no. Demanding them universally is wrong, and the thresholds are precise enough to encode as rules.

ApplicantIs an audit legally required?Source type
Proprietorship / partnership (non-company) Tax audit under s.44AB only if turnover exceeds ₹1 crore — raised to ₹10 crore when cash receipts and cash payments are each ≤5% of the total. Below that: no audit. Income Tax Act
LLP Audit only if turnover > ₹40 lakh or contribution > ₹25 lakh. Below both: no audit. LLP Act
Private / Public Limited company Always — statutory audit applies regardless of turnover. Companies Act

Fix: split the item in two.

  • Audited financial statements — required when entity_type ∈ {Private Limited, Public Limited}. Always available, so always fair to demand.
  • Financial statements (P&L and balance sheet) — required for everyone else, audited if available. A ₹60-lakh proprietorship can produce these; it cannot produce an audit report that legally doesn't exist.

The turnover-based rules (₹1cr / ₹10cr / ₹40L) are expressible too — we capture monthly turnover — but they need care, since our field is monthly and the thresholds are annual. Worth deciding whether to encode them or keep the simpler entity-based split.

Q3 · What does a Top-up genuinely need?

Your answer: it depends on what the top-up is on. Research agrees — and that has a design consequence.

A top-up is additional lending on an existing facility, so the question "what do we need?" resolves to "what is already on file, and what has gone stale?" Two things are always needed and are new to us:

  • Existing loan sanction letter — the terms being topped up
  • Existing loan statement of account — the repayment record

Beyond that, the answer genuinely varies with the underlying facility: a top-up on a LAP re-opens the property questions (has anything been mortgaged since?), while a top-up on an unsecured term loan mostly needs refreshed financials.

Design consequence: Docket already has a feature for exactly this — requirements can be marked reusable with a validity window (validityDays). Documents already collected on the borrower's earlier case can be carried forward while still valid, and only the expired ones re-requested. That is the honest way to model a top-up, and it needs no new concept — just correct validity periods per document, which is a question for your credit policy.

Q5 · Who supplies the valuation?

Verdict: the lender does — it must not be on the borrower's checklist. My draft guessed this; it is now confirmed.

RBI's prudential guidelines on collateral require lenders to obtain an independent valuation, and banks specifically will not accept a value supplied by the borrower or seller — the whole point of empanelment is that the valuer is the bank's, not the applicant's. The same logic applies to the legal opinion / title clearance report: Bank of Baroda's checklist explicitly refers to "the Bank's panel advocate".

Action: drop "Valuation report" from the proposed LAP list entirely. Asking a borrower for it would produce a document the lender cannot use — worse than not asking. If you ever want to track the panel valuer's report inside Docket, that is a different feature: an internally-uploaded document, not something requested from the applicant.

Q6 · Should GST documents become conditional on GST registration?

Your answer: yes. Straightforward to implement.

We capture GSTIN on the enquiry form, so the rule is expressible today. GST registration is compulsory above the turnover thresholds and voluntary below them, so a real applicant may legitimately have no GSTIN — which is exactly why the conditional is the correct shape rather than making it universally required.

One caveat worth your decision: the condition can key off "GSTIN present on the case" only if that field is reliably filled. Cases created from the website form carry it; cases created by staff may not, unless we make it a required field for GST-registered applicants.

Q7 · Should loan type become mandatory?

Your answer: depends on the loan type. Here is the mechanical consequence, which I'd ask you to weigh.

Loan type is optional today. If loan-type rules go live and a case is saved without one, every loan-specific document silently drops out of the checklist — the case looks complete while missing the property documents that make a LAP assessable. Silence is the dangerous part: nothing warns anyone.

Recommendation: make loan type required at case creation (a one-line config change, no code). If a case genuinely starts before the product is known, the honest modelling is an explicit "Not yet decided" option that asks only the common core — a deliberate state, rather than an empty field that quietly under-asks.

Q8 · Do different lenders need different lists?

Verdict: your instinct is half right — and the half that isn't matters commercially.

LayerVaries by lender?Why
KYC core (identity, address, PAN)NoFixed by RBI's KYC Master Direction. Every regulated lender asks the same thing.
Entity documentsBarelyDriven by company law, not credit policy.
Financial / credit layerYes, substantiallyBanks typically want 2–3 years of ITR plus audited financials; NBFCs commonly work off 6–12 months of bank statements and GST data, and some lend on small tickets without ITR at all.
Property / security layerYesEC period and NOC requirements are policy choices.

The good news is architectural: Docket already stores requirements per workflow, and workflows are per tenant. So an NBFC tenant and a bank tenant can run different checklists today with no code change. What we should ship is a well-researched default that a tenant can trim — not a single list imposed on everyone.

Beyond lending: the workflow library

Docket ships two workflows today (Business Loan, and a College Admission example). Every industry below is the same shape — a subject, a case, stages, and a conditional document checklist — so each is configuration, not code. Here are the three you named, researched, plus the ones that fit the pattern most naturally.

1 · CA firm — client engagement onboarding

ICAI issued Client KYC Norms for members in practice, applying to attest functions — audit, review, agreed-upon procedures and compilation of financial statements. Notably it is framed as information to be maintained rather than a heavy document list, which suits Docket well: the case fields carry the information, the checklist carries the evidence.

VocabularySubject = Client · Case = Engagement
Case fieldsEngagement type (Audit / Review / Compilation / Certification), entity type, PAN, CIN, DIN of directors, ultimate parent, previous auditor
ChecklistPAN of entity · identity proof of signatory · incorporation/registration certificate · MOA-AOA or partnership deed or LLP agreement · board resolution appointing the firm · previous year's financial statements · previous auditor's NOC (where an audit is being taken over) · GST registration · bank statements (engagement-dependent)
ConditionalsEntity documents gate on entity type; previous-auditor NOC gates on engagement type = Audit

2 · Company registration (SPICe+)

MCA's SPICe+ form is a single application delivering ten services — DIN, PAN, TAN, GSTIN, EPFO, ESIC and bank account among them. This is an unusually good fit for Docket because the document set is precisely defined by the filing itself.

VocabularySubject = Promoter · Case = Registration
Case fieldsProposed company name(s), company type (Pvt Ltd / OPC / LLP / Section 8), number of directors, authorised capital, registered-office state
ChecklistPAN of each subscriber/director · identity proof · residential address proof dated within 2 months (utility bill or bank statement) · passport photograph · Class 3 DSC for every director · DIN (where already held) · registered-office proof · NOC from the office premises owner · utility bill for the registered office · INC-9 declaration · MOA & AOA
ConditionalsDIN gates on "already holds DIN"; foreign-national documents (apostilled) gate on nationality

Note the freshness rule: the address proof must be no older than two months. Docket already models this — validityDays per requirement — so an expired utility bill would be flagged automatically rather than discovered at filing. That is a genuinely strong demo for this vertical.

3 · Employee onboarding

The most universally applicable workflow of the three, and the one with the clearest statutory backbone.

VocabularySubject = New joiner · Case = Onboarding
Case fieldsRole, department, joining date, monthly salary, employment type, has previous employment (Y/N)
ChecklistStatutory: PAN (TDS under s.192) · Aadhaar (EPFO KYC) · bank account proof · PF Form 11 declaration · Form 2 PF nomination · UAN (if previously employed) · ESI Form 1 (salary ≤ ₹21,000/month)
Credentials: degree certificates + marksheets · passport photograph
Previous employment: relieving letter · experience letter · last 3 months' payslips · Form 16
ConditionalsESI Form 1 gates on salary ≤ ₹21,000 · previous-employment block gates on "has previous employment" · UAN likewise

This one demonstrates conditional logic better than lending does: the ESI rule is a clean numeric threshold, and the entire previous-employment block appearing or vanishing on one answer is very visible in a demo.

The strategic point. Every workflow above needs zero engineering — a name, a vocabulary pair, stages, field definitions and a conditional checklist, all data. What each genuinely needs is domain review, exactly as the lending checklist does. That is the real constraint on Docket's breadth: not code, but someone who knows each field signing off the list. Worth saying plainly to Eswar, because it reframes "which industries do you support?" into "how fast can we configure one?" — and the answer is days, not releases.
